# SCM: Sleep-Consolidated Memory

## Quick read

5-page preview of a sleep-consolidation architecture with NREM + REM phases and value-based forgetting. Uses an importance-tagging step plus a self-model for introspection. All hand-coded; no learning. Benchmark is the author's own 8-test suite.

## Why it matters to us

Validates the sleep-consolidation framing more publicly, but the actual contribution is thin (no learning, no recognized benchmark). Useful as a citation showing the direction is "in the air" but not a competitor for any real number.

## Method in one paragraph

Working memory has limited capacity. Items are tagged on multiple importance dimensions as they enter. On a schedule, sleep phases run: NREM phase does memory consolidation (move important items to long-term), REM phase does abstraction (synthesize patterns). Value-based forgetting drops items below an importance threshold. A self-model layer lets the agent introspect on what it remembers.

## Results in numbers

- Perfect recall on 10-turn conversations (their benchmark)
- 90.9% noise reduction via adaptive forgetting
- <1ms search latency

## What they don't do

- No learned components
- No LoCoMo/LongMemEval/agent-env benchmarks
- Importance-tagging dimensions not enumerated
- Brief paper, less rigor than Auto-Dreamer or TiMem

## Abstract (verbatim, for reference)

> We present SCM (Sleep-Consolidated Memory), a research preview of a memory architecture for large language models that draws on neuroscientific principles. SCM implements five core components: a limited-capacity working memory, multi-dimensional importance tagging, offline sleep-stage consolidation with distinct NREM and REM phases, intentional value-based forgetting, and a computational self-model enabling introspection.
