# DeepPlanning: Benchmarking Long-Horizon Agentic Planning with Verifiable Constraints

## Quick read

The hardest published applied long-horizon planning benchmark as of 2026. Multi-day travel + multi-product shopping with cross-item constraints and coupon optimization. Top model (GPT-5.2-high) hits 44.6% average case accuracy — enormous headroom. Performance drops monotonically as horizon length (days in travel plan) increases.

## Why it matters to us

If we ship the contract/checkpoint orchestration system, this is the primary target. Beating GPT-5.2-high by 5-10pp on travel planning is a publishable result. The failure mode (cascading constraint violations across multi-step plans) is exactly what dynamic checkpoint-and-reallocation addresses.

## Method in one paragraph

Verifiable-constraint-shaped evaluation: each plan is checked against explicit hard constraints (budget, schedule overlaps, item compatibility, coupon eligibility). Case-level accuracy = 1 if all constraints satisfied, 0 otherwise. No LLM judge needed.

## Results in numbers

- GPT-5.2-high avg case acc: **44.6%**
- GPT-5.2-high travel case acc: **35.0%**
- Claude-4.5-Opus reasoning travel: **22.7%**
- Composite scores (constraint-level) higher: GPT-5.2 at 85.8, Claude at 75.1
- Drop: 7-day itineraries score substantially worse than 2-day

The gap between constraint-level (85.8) and case-level (35) reveals: models satisfy *most* constraints but fail to satisfy *all* — exactly the cascading-failure mode.

## What they don't do

- No middleware / orchestration layer studied — they test base models directly.
- No checkpoint or interrupt-and-replan capability evaluated.
- Cost not reported clearly.

## Open questions / followups

- What's a representative subset for cheap iteration? (Day-2 and Day-3 travel cases, probably.)
- How does verbose chain-of-thought compare to terse on these constraints?
