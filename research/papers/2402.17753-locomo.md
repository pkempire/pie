# LoCoMo: Evaluating Very Long-Term Conversational Memory of LLM Agents

## Quick read

10 long-form peer-to-peer conversations (Caroline-Melanie, etc.) with ~600 turns and ~200 questions each. Critical feature: every question is annotated with gold-evidence dia_ids (which turns the answer depends on). Categories: single_hop, multi_hop, temporal, adversarial, open_domain.

## Why it matters to us

Primary training set. The gold-evidence labels are what made write-time supervised training possible in mempol's original framing. Per-conversation splits also let us do honest train/eval separation.

## Method in one paragraph

Pre-generated dataset. Conversations have session timestamps, sessions have turns, turns have dia_ids. QA pairs reference specific dia_ids as evidence.

## Results in numbers

(No model results in the paper itself — it's a benchmark release.)

Our own conv-1 numbers under gpt-4.1 + gpt-5-mini judge:
- full_context: 75.1%
- naive_rag: 66.1%
- pie_temporal: 61.3% (v1) → 63.1% (v4 with strict-speaker extraction)

## What they don't do

- Adversarial category is poorly defined — many "adversarial" gold answers are actually positive facts ("Caroline's necklace symbolizes love, faith, and strength") not "abstain" cases.
- No control on retention budget.
- Doesn't measure long-horizon project work, only conversational chitchat.

## Open questions / followups

- How does conv-1 PIE generalize to convs 2-10? We only have conv-1 numbers.
- What's the right reweighting if we want to focus on multi_hop and temporal?
