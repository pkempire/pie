# Mem0: Building Production-Ready AI Agents with Scalable Long-Term Memory

## Quick read

The most-cited production memory system. Flat KV vector store; per-turn LLM-as-judge prompt picks one of ADD/UPDATE/DELETE/NONE. Their own paper reports a ~40% extraction-failure rate, which is the strongest single piece of evidence that hand-coded write logic is the bottleneck.

## Why it matters to us

Reference baseline. The "memory systems decide what to write with hand-tuned prompts" framing in our paper points at Mem0 first. If we ship a learned controller, Mem0's 40% failure rate is the headline target to improve.

## Method in one paragraph

Per turn: serialize recent context + the current Mem0 state → LLM prompt → returns one of ADD/UPDATE/DELETE/NONE plus the content. Apply the op to a vector store. At QA time: dense retrieval over the store, hand to answer LLM.

## Results in numbers

- LongMemEval: ~69% overall (gpt-4o judge)
- LoCoMo: ~27% on 50 questions (from our own re-run)
- Extraction-failure rate (self-reported): ~40%

## What they don't do

- No retention budget — store grows without bound.
- No learned write controller — every write decision is a fresh LLM call against the same prompt.
- No belief revision — UPDATE overwrites, doesn't reconcile.

## Open questions / followups

- What does the 40% extraction-failure rate translate to in QA accuracy lost?
- How much of the 31pp gap to Mastra-OM (94.87% LongMemEval) is extraction-fix vs control-policy-fix?
