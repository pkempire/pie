# GEPA: Reflective Prompt Evolution Can Outperform Reinforcement Learning

## Quick read

ICLR 2026 Oral from Khattab/Zaharia/Stoica/Klein/Sen/Dimakis/Potts (DSPy crew). A prompt optimizer that uses natural-language reflection on trajectories as the gradient signal instead of policy gradients on scalar rewards. Across 6 tasks: beats GRPO by 6% avg / 20% max with 35× fewer rollouts. Beats prior prompt-optimizer SOTA (MIPROv2) by >10%.

## How it works

For an AI system built from one or more LLM prompts:
1. Sample trajectories on the task.
2. LLM reflects on failed trajectories in natural language — diagnoses what went wrong, proposes specific prompt edits.
3. Test the proposed prompt on a small batch.
4. Maintain a **Pareto frontier** of prompt variants — different prompts win on different sub-distributions of the task. Don't collapse to one best.
5. Use a **genetic** operator to combine complementary lessons from frontier prompts into new candidates.
6. Iterate.

The core philosophical claim: a scalar reward is one bit per rollout; a natural-language critique is hundreds of bits per rollout. For matched compute budget, reflection-based search extracts much more signal per sample.

## Why it matters to our work

Direct application: optimize the [[sleep-consolidation|consolidator]] prompt with GEPA instead of GRPO. See [[01-gepa-consolidator-on-locomo|Goal 01]] for the active experiment.

If GEPA matches [[2605.20616-auto-dreamer|Auto-Dreamer]]'s quality at 1/35th the compute, the practical implication is huge: consolidation training becomes laptop-budget instead of 8× H100. See [[02-autodreamer-reproduction|Goal 02]] for that downstream experiment.

## What it can't do

- Change model knowledge or fundamental capability
- Train models for non-verifiable open-ended generation
- Run during deployment (offline optimizer, like any)
