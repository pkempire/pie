# Hindsight is 20/20

## Quick read

Vectorize.io's memory system. Three-op architecture (retain/recall/reflect) over a temporal knowledge graph with preference-conditioned reasoning. Tested with Gemini-3 backbone. The interesting kink: their simpler baseline (Backboard, 90.0%) marginally beats their full system (89.61%). That's a publication-honest result that quietly suggests most of the complexity isn't load-bearing.

## Numbers

- Hindsight (Gemini-3): **89.61%** on LoCoMo ✅ abstract-verified verbatim
- Hindsight (open 20B): 39% → 83.6% over full-context baseline (same backbone)
- LongMemEval: 91.4% (scaled backbone)
- Backboard (simpler baseline): 90.0% (table-only)
- Memobase v0.0.37: 75.78% (cited in abstract as prior strongest open system)

## What's interesting

The Backboard-beats-Hindsight result is the kind of finding most papers bury. It implies the "retain/recall/reflect" structure adds ~zero on this benchmark vs a clean RAG baseline at the Gemini-3 scale. Two readings: (a) at frontier-model capability, structured memory matters less; (b) the eval is too easy at this scale.

Both readings point to the same product question: **does memory architecture sophistication matter when the base model is good enough?** This is the question that should haunt our work.
