The Shape of Memory

Give an AI a past and you face two questions the field keeps tangling together: where what it knows lives, and when it does the work of organizing it. Placed on those axes, every method — vector database to trained model — sorts into three tiers, and the crowding tells you why agents can't yet learn.

Each point is an approach with its original source; products underneath are examples. ★ = worth knowing by name.

Two axes, three tiers

Vertical = where knowledge lives: re-read text (bottom), a pre-computed KV cache (middle), or trained weights (top). Horizontal = when you organize it, from ingest (left) to query (right). The tier's color is the access you need to build there — the gate that explains the crowding. Hover any point for how it works, its source, and where you've seen it.

Why the bottom row is packed. The token tier is the only one you can build on a closed API, so every funded company lives there, differentiated mostly by how it compresses text. The cache and weight tiers were gated behind open weights — a gate now falling as GLM-, Qwen- and Llama-class models get strong and cheap, and methods like Cartridges make corpus-specific memory practical. The sparse upper tiers are where memory stops being retrieval and starts becoming learning.
