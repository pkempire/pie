The Shape of Memory

Give an AI a past and you must answer two questions the field keeps tangling together: where what it knows physically lives, and when it does the work of organizing it. Plot both and every method — from a vector database to a trained model — sorts into three tiers. The reason almost everyone piles into one of them is the same reason agents can't yet learn — and that reason is starting to fall away.

Three tiers, one question

Down the side, where knowledge lives: as re-read text, as a pre-computed KV cache, or trained into weights. Across the top, when you pay to organize it — at ingest, at query, or both. Each tier's color is the access you need to build there. That gate — not merit — is why the map looks the way it does. Hover any approach for how it actually works, its original source, and where you've seen it.

Why the map is lopsided — and why that's the opening. The token tier is packed because it's the only one you can build on a closed API, so every funded company is there, differentiated mostly by how cleverly it compresses text. The cache and weight tiers were gated behind needing open weights — but that gate is falling fast: GLM-, Qwen- and Llama-class open models are now strong and cheap to self-host, and methods like Cartridges make corpus-specific memory practical (in-context quality at ~38× less memory). The two sparse tiers aren't dead ends. They're where memory stops being retrieval and starts becoming learning — and they just became reachable.
